Methodology

From product level cash flows to a per share fair value

This page describes how each pipeline product is modelled, how probability weighting is applied, and how the product rNPVs are aggregated into a parent-attributable equity value and a per-share value.

The rNPV formula

For each product, the risk-adjusted NPV is:

\[rNPV = PoS_{cum} \; \times \; \sum_{t=T_0}^{T_{end}} \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^{t - t_0 + 0.5}} \; - \; \sum_{t=t_0}^{T_0-1} \frac{w(phase_t) \; \cdot \; DevCost_{phase_t}}{(1+r)^{t - t_0 + 0.5}}\]

Where:

  • \(CF_t\) = operating cash flow in year \(t\) = \(Sales_t \times (\text{OpMargin}_t - \text{Royalties}_t)\). The margin is a time-varying curve (see Operating margin below).
  • \(PoS_{cum}\) = cumulative probability that the product reaches the market from its current phase (product of transitions, see Probability of success below).
  • \(r\) = discount rate (biotech WACC), mid-year convention.
  • \(DevCost_{phase_t}\) = clinical development cost of the phase that is spent in year \(t\).
  • \(w(phase_t)\) = cumulative probability of reaching that phase (from the current phase). The current phase has \(w = 1\) (certain); next phase has $w = $ one transition; the one after has $w = $ two transitions in product; etc.
  • \(T_0\) = launch year, \(T_{end}\) = end of exclusivity window.

Probability of success (PoS)

Phase-to-phase transition probabilities are from BIO/Informa/QLS, Clinical Development Success Rates and Contributing Factors 2011-2020 (2021), all-indication figures. These are the single-source replacement for the mixed set of PoS numbers used in v1.0, which combined BIO’s filing-to-approval figure with a Biomedtracker Phase III-to-approval figure under a WSL label. Wong, Siah & Lo (2019) subsequently published a corrigendum on the Phase 2→3 and Phase 3→approval columns of its Table 1; we therefore use BIO 2021 as the single reference.

Phase-to-phase transitions (BIO 2021):

transition probability
Phase 1 → Phase 2 52.0 %
Phase 2 → Phase 3 28.9 %
Phase 3 → NDA/BLA 57.8 %
NDA/BLA → approval 90.6 %

Cumulative PoS to approval (derived):

The cumulative LOA from Phase 1 is 7.9 % per BIO’s published figure and matches our product-of-transitions. Phase-specific overrides can be applied per product in src/assumptions.py; the base case uses the cumulative benchmarks above.

Revenue curve

For each product we model annual sales as:

  1. Ramp phase, linear buildup from the latest anchored sales figure (historical or guidance) to peak. Ramp durations are set per product in src/assumptions.py (QUVIVIQ 8 years from 2022 launch; lucerastat 4 years; early-stage 5-6 years). We do not use a universal “5-7 years” assumption; each ramp is justified in the product entry.
  2. Peak phase, plateau at the estimated peak sales figure.
  3. Patent cliff, −85 % in the year after expiry with 30 % annual decay thereafter (orphan assets decay slower; broadly consistent).

Peak sales for marketed and Phase 3 assets are triangulated from company guidance, published comparables, and epidemiology data; for Phase 2 assets they are explicit PLACEHOLDER values that will be reanchored after each programme’s proof-of-concept readout. Each peak sales value is documented in src/assumptions.py with its source and status.

Operating margin and cash flow

Sales are converted to operating cash flow via a time-varying operating margin, parameterised per product by op_margin_at_launch and op_margin_at_peak, interpolated linearly between the two anchor years. For QUVIVIQ in particular the margin ramps from 5 % in 2026 (Idorsia’s commercial-profitability target year) to 30 % at peak in 2030, rather than a flat 30 % from 2026.

Working capital and capex are treated as netting to zero over the forecast horizon (standard rNPV simplification, discussed in Limitations). Taxes are proxied by the operating margin itself.

Development costs

Development costs are phase-weighted:

  • Current phase: remaining cost is certain (weight = 1). The amount is set per product in remaining_current_phase_cost_chf_m using an Idorsia-scale historical burn (~CHF 24 M/year for Phase 2 PoC; see TODO_DATA.md for status).
  • Each subsequent phase: cost is weighted by the cumulative probability of reaching that phase (product of BIO 2021 transitions). Future-phase spending does not start until after the current-phase readout.
  • Industry-benchmark unweighted phase costs (DiMasi 2016, converted USD 2013 → CHF 2026 by US CPI 1.40 × USD/CHF 0.90 ≈ 1.26) are the default DEV_COSTS_BY_PHASE_CHF_M. Products may override specific phases (e.g. lucerastat’s orphan Fabry Phase 3 at CHF 100 M vs the DiMasi all-indication CHF 322 M average).

This replaces the v1.0 convention, which treated all remaining dev costs as certain regardless of future-phase advancement probability (and so penalised Phase 1/2 assets by the full future dev spend rather than its expected value).

Discount rate

A biotech-appropriate WACC in the 11-13 % range is used. The base case is 12 %; sensitivities stress at 10 % and 14 %.

Central costs (unallocated G&A + upstream research)

In addition to product-level dev costs, the parent carries unallocated central costs that are not captured in op_margin:

  • G&A (holding, corporate, finance): CHF 71 M in 2025 non-GAAP (AR 2025, SG&A breakdown).
  • Upstream research (preclinical, platform, non-program-specific): placeholder at CHF 25 M/year (~27 % of total non-GAAP R&D), listed in TODO_DATA.md.

Trajectory: decline 10 %/year to a floor of CHF 50 M by year ~8, plateau to 2040, then scale with the model’s PoS-weighted revenue (floor CHF 10 M) through 2050. The present value is deducted in the equity-value bridge.

Modelling conventions

Two non-obvious choices sit under the rNPV that would produce materially different numbers if flipped. Both are documented here for transparency.

Discounting convention: mid-year

Cash flows in year \(t\) are discounted by \((1+r)^{-(t - t_0 + 0.5)}\), the mid-year convention. This is the textbook rNPV default: cash is assumed to flow evenly through the year.

convention equity value (CHF M) value per share (CHF) Δ equity value vs mid year Δ per share vs mid year
Start of year -609 0 +1 0
Mid year (used here) -611 0 +0 0
End of year -612 0 -1 0

The convention flips both the asset side (product rNPVs) and the net-cash side; the per-share effect is amplified by the net-debt position.

QUVIVIQ ramp trajectory: 8 years to peak

The model interpolates linearly from the last anchored sales figure (CHF 200 M guidance for 2026) to the peak of CHF 600 M by 2030 — an 8-year ramp counted from the 2022 launch year. This produces the sequence 200 → 300 → 400 → 500 → 600 (2026-2030), consistent with the ~+50 % YoY growth Idorsia has been delivering and guides for 2026.

An earlier draft used a 6-year ramp (peak in 2028), implying 200 → 400 → 600 in two years. Defensible only under an aggressive combined US descheduling + pediatric approval + European reimbursement bull case.

What is NOT modelled

Consistent with the “order of magnitude” tool ambition:

  • Taxes, subsumed into the operating margin. A separate effective tax layer would trim equity value by ~10-20 %.
  • Working capital and capex, netted to zero over the forecast horizon.
  • Product outcome correlation, treated as independent (no portfolio effect).
  • J&J convertible optionality, the CHF 335 M convertible loan is treated as pure debt. An if-converted test applies in scenarios where V/share exceeds the CHF ~11.5 strike (adding 29.1 M shares, removing CHF 335 M of debt). See Scenarios in Results.
  • SARL cash flow priorities, the ring-fenced debt at Idorsia Investments SARL is excluded from the parent bridge in the base case; the residual reversion to the parent is set to zero and stressed only in scenarios.
  • Central costs modelled as a simple decline-then-floor-then-proportional curve, without a line-by-line projection of G&A or upstream research.
  • Future value of unborn pipeline candidates, the upstream research line is deducted but no value is attributed to the compounds that upstream research will generate. This biases the model downward.
  • QUVIVIQ post-launch R&D, pediatric programme and label extensions are in neither dev_cost_curve (prelaunch only) nor central_costs (non-program-specific). See Limitations.

Aggregation

\[EquityValue_{parent} = \sum_{i \in products} rNPV_i + NetCash_{parent} + SARL_{residual} - PV(CentralCosts)\]

\[\text{Value per share} = \max\!\left(0, \; \frac{EquityValue_{parent}}{\text{Shares outstanding}}\right)\]

The max(0, ·) floor reflects limited liability: shareholders cannot owe money to the company. The raw (potentially negative) per-share value is kept as value_per_share_raw_chf for display in notes.

Sensitivity

The model is stress-tested along the axes that dominate rNPV in practice:

  • Discount rate (10 % → 14 %)
  • PoS of the lead clinical candidate (±20 %)
  • Peak sales of the top revenue driver (±30 %)
  • SARL residual (0 → CHF 400 M)
  • QUVIVIQ operating margin (±5 pp at peak)
  • Central costs upstream placeholder (±50 %)

Limitations

  • rNPV is an order-of-magnitude tool, not a precise valuation.
  • Product outcomes are assumed independent (no portfolio correlation).
  • Working capital and capex netted to zero.
  • No explicit tax treatment beyond the operating margin.
  • Sensitive to peak sales assumptions, especially for Phase 2 placeholders.
  • Upstream research is deducted without attributing value to the compounds it generates — a downward bias.
  • QUVIVIQ post-launch R&D is not modelled.
