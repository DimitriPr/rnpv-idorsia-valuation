Methodology

From product level cash flows to a per share fair value

This page describes how each pipeline product is modelled, how probability weighting is applied, and how the product rNPVs are aggregated into a parent-attributable equity value and a per-share value.

The rNPV formula

For each product, the risk-adjusted NPV is:

\[rNPV = PoS_{cum} \; \times \; \sum_{t=T_0}^{T_{end}} \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^{\tau(t)}} \; - \; \sum_{t=t_0}^{T_0-1} \frac{w(phase_t) \; \cdot \; DevCost_{phase_t}}{(1+r)^{\tau(t)}}\]

Where:

  • \(CF_t\) = operating cash flow in year \(t\) = \(Sales_t \times \text{ContributionMargin}_t\), with contribution margin = \((Sales - COGS - Selling)/Sales\). G&A and R&D are deducted once elsewhere (as PV of central costs and PV of development costs). The margin is a time-varying curve (see Contribution margin below).
  • \(PoS_{cum}\) = cumulative probability that the product reaches the market from its current phase (product of transitions, see Probability of success below).
  • \(r\) = discount rate (biotech WACC), mid-year valuation-date convention.
  • \(DevCost_{phase_t}\) = clinical development cost of the phase that is spent in year \(t\).
  • \(w(phase_t)\) = cumulative probability of reaching that phase (from the current phase). The current phase has \(w = 1\) (certain); next phase has $w = $ one transition; the one after has $w = $ two transitions in product; etc.
  • \(T_0\) = launch year, \(T_{end}\) = end of forecast horizon.
  • \(\tau(t)\) = discount exponent under the mid-year valuation-date convention: 0.25 for $t = $ valuation year (H2 2026 stub, half flow at 3-month midpoint), \((t - t_0)\) for $t > $ valuation year. The valuation year flow is prorated by 0.5 to reflect H2-only cash.

Idorsia pays a central residual royalty to J&J on net sales of all products until a cumulative cap is reached (H1 2026 Report note 4). This is modelled as a central line (not a per-product haircut) in the equity-value bridge below. The remaining cap at 30 Jun 2026 is CHF 272 M.

Probability of success (PoS)

Phase-to-phase transition probabilities are from BIO/Informa/QLS, Clinical Development Success Rates and Contributing Factors 2011-2020 (2021), all-indication figures. These are the single-source replacement for the mixed set of PoS numbers used in v1.0, which combined BIO's filing-to-approval figure with a Biomedtracker Phase III-to-approval figure under a WSL label. Wong, Siah & Lo (2019) subsequently published a corrigendum on the Phase 2->3 and Phase 3->approval columns of its Table 1; we therefore use BIO 2021 as the single reference.

Phase-to-phase transitions (BIO 2021):

transition probability
Phase 1 -> Phase 2 52.0 %
Phase 2 -> Phase 3 28.9 %
Phase 3 -> NDA/BLA 57.8 %
NDA/BLA -> approval 90.6 %

Cumulative PoS to approval (derived):

Phase 1 7.9 %
Phase 2 15.1 %

The cumulative LOA from Phase 1 is 7.9 % per BIO's published figure and matches our product-of-transitions. Phase-specific overrides can be applied per product in src/assumptions.py; the base case uses the cumulative benchmarks above.

Revenue curve

For each product we model annual sales as:

  1. Ramp phase, linear buildup from the latest anchored sales figure (historical or guidance) to peak. Ramp durations are set per product in src/assumptions.py (QUVIVIQ 8 years from 2022 launch; lucerastat 4 years; early-stage 5-6 years). We do not use a universal "5-7 years" assumption; each ramp is justified in the product entry.
  2. Peak phase, plateau at the estimated peak sales figure.
  3. Patent cliff, -85 % in the year after expiry with 30 % annual decay thereafter (orphan assets decay slower; broadly consistent).

Peak sales for marketed and Phase 3 assets are triangulated from company guidance, published comparables, and epidemiology data; for Phase 2 assets they are explicit PLACEHOLDER values that will be reanchored after each programme's proof-of-concept readout. Each peak sales value is documented in src/assumptions.py with its source and status.

Contribution margin and cash flow

Sales are converted to operating cash flow via a time-varying contribution margin, parameterised per product by op_margin_at_launch and op_margin_at_peak, interpolated linearly between the two anchor years. The contribution-margin convention is \((Sales - COGS - Selling) / Sales\), so G&A and R&D are not inside the product line (they are deducted once each, as PV of central costs and PV of development costs). For QUVIVIQ in particular the contribution margin ramps from 9 % in 2026 (sourced on H1 2026 actuals: \((97 - 19 - 69)/97\) per H1 p.3 / p.6) to 55 % at peak in 2030 (COGS 12-20 %, Selling 25-30 % at steady state).

Working capital and capex are netted to zero over the forecast horizon (standard rNPV simplification, discussed in Limitations). Taxes are proxied by the contribution margin itself.

Development costs

Development costs are phase-weighted:

  • Current phase: remaining cost is certain (weight = 1). The amount is set per product in remaining_current_phase_cost_chf_m. For the Phase 2 PoC programs (CCR6, CXCR7, CXCR3) this value is calibrated downward from DiMasi 2016 Phase 2 averages: the H1 2026 Report (p.7) explicitly attributes the 2026 clinical dev spend to the late-stage daridorexant and lucerastat programs, so applying full-program DiMasi anchors would double-count. The 2026 model development total is calibrated to the implicit guidance decomposition (CHF 60 M = 330 total - 39 COGS - 143 selling - 54 G&A - 34 upstream research). Lucerastat Phase 3 remains anchored on the orphan Fabry registrational trial estimate (CHF 100 M).
  • Each subsequent phase: cost is weighted by the cumulative probability of reaching that phase (product of BIO 2021 transitions). Future-phase spending does not start until after the current-phase readout.
  • Industry-benchmark unweighted phase costs (DiMasi 2016, converted USD 2013 to CHF 2026 by US CPI 1.40 x USD/CHF 0.90 ~ 1.26) are the default DEV_COSTS_BY_PHASE_CHF_M. Products may override specific phases.

This replaces the v1.0 convention, which treated all remaining dev costs as certain regardless of future-phase advancement probability (and so penalised Phase 1/2 assets by the full future dev spend rather than its expected value).

Discount rate

A biotech-appropriate WACC in the 11-13 % range is used. The base case is 12 %; sensitivities stress at 10 % and 14 %.

Central costs (unallocated G&A + upstream research)

In addition to product-level dev costs, the parent carries unallocated central costs that are not captured in the contribution margin:

  • G&A (holding, corporate, finance): CHF 54 M / year annualised (H1 2026 Report p.6, status sourced).
  • Upstream research (preclinical, biology, chemistry): CHF 34 M / year annualised (H1 2026 Report p.7, status sourced).

Trajectory: decline 10 % / year to a floor of CHF 50 M by 2040, plateau to that year, then scale with the model's PoS-weighted revenue (hard floor CHF 10 M) through 2050. The present value is deducted in the equity-value bridge.

J&J residual royalty

Independently of the SARL carve-out and of the J&J convertible loan, Idorsia pays Janssen Pharmaceuticals (now part of J&J) a residual royalty on net sales of all Idorsia products until a cumulative cap is reached (H1 2026 Report note 4, p.32). The disclosure is "low-to-mid single-digit royalty on net sales of all products, cap of CHF 272 M remaining at 30 Jun 2026". We model:

  • Rate: 4 % of PoS-weighted aggregate net sales (status assumption).
  • Cap: CHF 272 M remaining (status sourced).
  • Application: central line in the equity-value bridge, deducted once at PV. Not a per-product haircut.

This replaces the v1.0 / v1.1 convention which carried the royalty as a per-product margin haircut (and so compounded the cap effect across products).

Modelling conventions

Two non-obvious choices sit under the rNPV that would produce materially different numbers if flipped. Both are documented here for transparency.

Discounting convention: mid-year valuation date (30 Jun 2026)

Cash flows are discounted from the valuation date of 30 June 2026, i.e. mid-year of the valuation year. The convention is:

  • \(t\) = valuation year (2026): discount exponent 0.25 (H2 2026 stub, midpoint of the H2 at 3 months out); the valuation-year cash flow is also prorated by 0.5 to reflect H2 only.
  • \(t\) = valuation year + \(n\): discount exponent \(n\).

This is a tighter version of the textbook mid-year convention: it anchors the entire bridge (asset side AND net cash side) on the H1 2026 Report balance sheet date, rather than discounting cash flows that were already included in the balance sheet.

convention equity value (CHF M) value per share (CHF) delta equity vs base delta per share vs base
Start of year 748 2.45 -2 -0.01
Mid year (textbook, offset 0.5) 692 2.27 -57 -0.19
Mid-year valuation date (used here) 750 2.46 +0 0
End of year 640 2.1 -110 -0.36

The convention flips both the asset side (product rNPVs) and the net-cash side; the per-share effect is amplified by the net-debt position.

QUVIVIQ ramp trajectory: 8 years to peak

The model interpolates linearly from the last anchored sales figure (CHF 200 M guidance for 2026) to the peak of CHF 600 M by 2030, an 8-year ramp counted from the 2022 launch year. This produces the sequence 200 -> 300 -> 400 -> 500 -> 600 (2026-2030), consistent with the roughly +50 % YoY growth Idorsia has been delivering and guides for 2026.

An earlier draft used a 6-year ramp (peak in 2028), implying 200 -> 400 -> 600 in two years. Defensible only under an aggressive combined US descheduling + pediatric approval + European reimbursement bull case.

What is NOT modelled

Consistent with the "order of magnitude" tool ambition:

  • Taxes, subsumed into the contribution margin. A separate effective tax layer would trim equity value by roughly 10-20 %.
  • Working capital and capex, netted to zero over the forecast horizon.
  • Product outcome correlation, treated as independent (no portfolio effect).
  • J&J convertible loan optionality: the remaining CHF 250 M principal is treated as a deterministic equity issuance of 21.7 M shares at CHF 11.48, not as debt. Pharmakon's covenant (H1 p.44) forbids cash repayment of a convertible loan, so the base case assumes shares at maturity. See Scenarios in Results; the J&J principal does not have an if-converted test (it is mandatory).
  • CB 2034 if-converted test (strike CHF 6.00) is applied scenario-by-scenario: triggers when raw V/share > 6, adding 2.72 M shares and removing CHF 16.5 M of debt.
  • SARL cash flow priorities: the ring-fenced debt at Idorsia Investments SARL is excluded from the parent bridge in the base case; the residual reversion to the parent is set to zero and stressed only in scenarios. SARL notes A1 / A2 carry a limited and subordinated guarantee from Idorsia Ltd (H1 p.17, p.41); the base case ignores that contingent liability, flagged in Limitations.
  • Pharmakon loan covenants beyond the drawn amount (minimum-liquidity, minimum-revenue, obligation to strengthen liquidity within an agreed timeframe) are not modelled. The implicit equity raise is not in the share count.
  • Ex-Idorsia-territory partner economics (Mochida Japan, Simcere China, Idorsia Korea) are not projected forward. Current contribution (contract revenue H1 2026 = CHF 8 M) is reported in the calibration.
  • Central costs modelled as a simple decline-then-floor-then-proportional curve, without a line-by-line projection of G&A or upstream research.
  • Future value of unborn pipeline candidates: the upstream research line is deducted but no value is attributed to the compounds that upstream research will generate. Downward bias.
  • QUVIVIQ lifecycle R&D (pediatric programme, label extensions, dosage optimisation) is not a separate product; it is implicitly covered by the 2026 development total calibrated to guidance. Post-2026 lifecycle spend is not projected.

Aggregation

\[EquityValue_{parent} = \sum_{i \in products} rNPV_i + NetCash_{parent} + SARL_{residual} - PV(CentralCosts) - PV(J\&JRoyalty)\]

\[\text{Value per share} = \max\!\left(0, \; \frac{EquityValue_{parent}}{\text{Diluted valuation-basis shares}}\right)\]

The diluted valuation basis is: issued shares (263.1 M) + J&J mandatory conversion (21.7 M) + warrants (10.85 M, assumed exercised, strike CHF 1.49) + unvested free shares (9.5 M) = 305.15 M shares. CB 2034 (+2.72 M, strike CHF 6.00) is added when the if-converted test triggers. Options (7.1 M at CHF 10.97) are informational and excluded from the valuation denominator (strike well above base case).

The max(0, .) floor reflects limited liability: shareholders cannot owe money to the company. The raw (potentially negative) per-share value is kept as value_per_share_raw_chf for display.

Sensitivity

The model is stress-tested along the axes that dominate rNPV in practice:

  • Discount rate (10 % to 14 %)
  • QUVIVIQ peak sales (+/-30 %)
  • Lucerastat peak sales (+/-30 %)
  • QUVIVIQ peak contribution margin (+/-10 pp)
  • Lucerastat peak contribution margin (+/-10 pp)
  • Lucerastat PoS (+/-20 %)
  • SARL residual (0 vs CHF 400 M)
  • Total central costs, G&A + upstream (+/-25 %)

Limitations

  • rNPV is an order-of-magnitude tool, not a precise valuation.
  • Product outcomes are assumed independent (no portfolio correlation).
  • Working capital and capex netted to zero.
  • No explicit tax treatment beyond the contribution margin.
  • Sensitive to peak sales and peak-margin assumptions, especially for Phase 2 placeholders.
  • Pharmakon covenant-driven equity raise is not in the share count.
  • SARL note A1 / A2 limited guarantee is excluded from the base case.
  • J&J residual royalty rate is an assumption (disclosure is "low-to-mid single digit"); the cap and the mechanic are sourced.
  • Upstream research is deducted without attributing value to the compounds it generates, a downward bias.
  • QUVIVIQ lifecycle R&D is implicit in the 2026 total; not projected beyond 2026.
